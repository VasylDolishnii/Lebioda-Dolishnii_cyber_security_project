## Slide 3: TASK 3

I would review the invoice, VAT treatment, contractual documentation, and any supporting records to determine whether the customer's claim is valid. I would involve the Tax team to confirm the correct VAT treatment, collaborate with Finance and AR to verify transaction details, and engage Sales to support customer communication. If an error is identified, I would arrange for a corrected invoice; if not, I would provide clear supporting documentation to the customer. Throughout the process, I would maintain proactive, transparent communication and provide regular updates until the issue is resolved.

My first action would be to analyze the aging report by country, customer, and overdue category to identify the main drivers of the overdue portfolio. I would then focus on the largest and oldest outstanding balances, understand the root causes of non-payment, and implement a structured collection and escalation plan. Collections would be prioritized based on value, aging, customer importance, and likelihood of recovery. On a weekly basis, I would monitor DSO, overdue balances over 60 days, cash collections against target, Collection Effectiveness Index (CEI), and open dispute values.

### SCENARIO 1
I would investigate whether the increase is driven by higher dispute volumes, invoicing delays, weaker collection activities, changes in customer payment behavior, or overly generous credit terms. To reduce DSO, I would strengthen collection processes and escalation procedures, improve invoice accuracy and dispute resolution times, and review credit limits and payment terms for higher-risk customers. Progress would be measured through DSO reduction, improved cash collections, lower overdue balances, and a decrease in open disputes.

## Slide 4: TASK 4

Overdue AR = €2M + €1M + €1M = €4M , 40% of AR balances are already overdue
90+ days bucket represents the highest risk area. These balances may indicate customer financial difficulties, unresolved disputes, poor collection follow-up, or potential bad debts.
60–90 days bucket requires close monitoring and targeted collection efforts
30–60 days bucket represents half of the overdue balances, this requires immediate action as this can become more dificult to collect.
I would also review which customers are driving the overdue balances, identify any disputed invoices, and analyze overdue trends by customer and country to understand the main risks and collection priorities.
Provision – I would apply the IFRS 9 ECL model, which requires analyzing historical write-off rates by aging bucket. The highest rate would apply to 90+ days, a moderate rate to 60–90 days, a lower rate to 30–60 days, and the lowest rate to current balances. There may also be pending write-offs or other items requiring individual assessment, resulting in a specific provision. Total bad debt provision = ECL + specific provision.
